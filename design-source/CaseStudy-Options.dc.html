Problem

Options are complex and can expire worthless. Awaed needed them to make sense for retail investors, not only for pros.

01 · Context

Options for retail investors, on the Web Trader foundation

Awaed added US options trading, and it had to move fast. The audience was retail investors, many of them new to options. I designed it for both Awaed Web, built on the Web Trader system, and the mobile app. As the lead and only designer, I owned the experience end to end, working closely with the PM.

02 · The challenge

A complex, high-risk product for first-time traders

Options can expire worthless, and many of our users had never traded them. The experience had to make the risk clear without scaring people away, while following Shariah rules and Awaed’s own trading rules.

03 · Key decisions

Letting the objective decide

Decision 01

It starts with who gets in

Eligibility is the first thing a user meets, so the experience starts there: a short questionnaire on market direction, risk, a knowledge check and risk tolerance, ending in a clear eligibility result. Once the business confirmed the direction, I designed the full experience in one week, section by section, and worked through the PRD so every rule and edge case had a designed state.

Decision 02

Two directions, tested in a one-day prototype

Direction A showed one table at a time, calls or puts. Direction B put calls and puts side by side, the way professional platforms do. I built a prototype of both in a day, so the business could compare them in use instead of imagining them.

Decision 03

One table, because we were designing for retail

We were targeting retail users, so one table made more sense: it keeps the choice to a single question, do you expect the price to rise or fall? For more advanced traders, I added two things to the roadmap: a toggle to switch to the side-by-side view, and a strategy builder.

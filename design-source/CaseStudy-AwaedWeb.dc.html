01 · Context

A web trader other products could build on

Awaed wanted a web app for advanced traders, and it had to be the foundation for a growing line of products. Options trading and an advanced web trader came first, with more on the roadmap. The goal was to build it fast. As the lead designer, I owned it end to end, and with no PM on the project, I also shaped what we built and in what order.

02 · The challenge

Two languages, two markets, and no source of truth

On mobile, the designs existed in several versions. Trading rules were scattered with nowhere to check them: US market hours, limit prices, order volume, KSA regulations. There were no unified order summaries or order flows. Before I could design the web, someone had to work out what the rules actually were.

03 · How I worked

Turning ambiguity into clear requirements

With no PM available for the project, I took on gathering the requirements, drawing on the PMs who had shaped the mobile app and the developers who built it. I documented every case and got it confirmed by PM and development before handoff. During the build I kept a direct line open with the engineering team, documented everything in the file, and handed over a structure they were happy to build from.

04 · Key decisions

How I turned scattered rules into a system

Decision 01

What was live on mobile came first

I prioritised the roadmap by what was already released on mobile. Those features went into the web MVP; anything still in development on mobile waited for the next phase. That let me put quality first without falling short on the scope the MVP needed.

Decision 02

Every rule designed as a real state

I went to the PMs who had worked on these features before, and to the developers who built mobile, to see how each rule was really implemented. Then I designed every case as a state in the system, in English and Arabic, all built from components. PM and engineering confirmed it, and engineering built the web from that file.

Decision 03

One set of tokens, switched by market

Saudi and US trade differently: tick sizes and fluctuation limits on one side, penny-stock and pre-market rules on the other. Market modes let one screen carry both.
